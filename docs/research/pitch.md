## FINDINGS
# Pitch Detection Research for High-Accuracy Guitar Tuner (Flutter/Android, down to F#1 ≈ 46.25 Hz)

## 1. Algorithm comparison (monophonic guitar tuning)

**YIN (de Cheveigné & Kawahara 2002)** — time-domain cumulative-mean-normalized difference function (CMNDF) with an absolute threshold (canonical 0.1) that picks the FIRST dip below threshold, then parabolic interpolation. This "first dip" rule is exactly why YIN is octave-robust on low strings: even when the 2nd/3rd harmonics dominate the fundamental (typical for E2/B1/A1/F#1 through phone mics, which roll off below ~100 Hz), the dip at the true period still exists and is found before deeper dips at multiples. Reported ~3x fewer gross errors than plain ACF. Naive cost O(W·τmax) ≈ O((W/2)²); the FFT variant ("YIN-FFT", used by aubio and sevagh/pitch-detection) is O(N log N). Real-world commercial validation: ChromTuner (a native macOS ±1-cent tuner) documents exactly this stack — YIN, 4096-sample window, 50% overlap, ~22 Hz update, 70 Hz HPF, octave-error correction for plucked strings, median-of-5 + exponential smoothing, claiming ±0.14¢ on sines and ±1¢ real-world.

**MPM (McLeod Pitch Method, 2005)** — normalized square difference function (NSDF) computed via FFT autocorrelation; picks among key maxima using a "clarity/k" threshold (paper uses 0.8–1.0, typically 0.8–0.93): take the highest maxima, then choose the FIRST maximum within k of it, plus parabolic interpolation. Explicitly designed for real-time guitar/violin tuning (the Tartini tuner); the k-threshold rule is its built-in octave-error defense, and the NSDF clarity value (0–1) doubles as a free confidence metric. In sevagh/pitch-detection's degradation tests MPM slightly edges YIN in noisy conditions; the two are otherwise comparable. CPU: same order as YIN-FFT (a few FFTs per frame).

**pYIN (Mauch & Dixon 2014)** — replaces YIN's single threshold with a threshold distribution producing multiple F0 candidates per frame, then Viterbi-decodes an HMM across frames. Best-in-class for offline transcription, but Viterbi is inherently non-causal: real-time use requires truncated/short-time Viterbi with look-ahead, adding latency and "truncation error." CPU is several times YIN plus HMM state upkeep. For a tuner, the same stability is achieved cheaper with YIN/MPM + confidence gating + median filtering. Also the reference implementation (c4dm/pyin Vamp plugin) is GPL.

**Plain autocorrelation (ACF)** — cheapest conceptually but the worst octave behavior: picks lag 2T when the ACF peak at the octave-below is taller (common with strong even harmonics), and octave-high when the fundamental is weak. Needs ad-hoc peak-picking heuristics that YIN/MPM formalize better. Not recommended as the primary method.

**FFT-peak / spectral methods** — unsuitable alone for low strings on two counts: (a) resolution — at N=4096/48 kHz the bin spacing is 11.7 Hz, i.e. ~4 semitones of ambiguity at 46 Hz; even with parabolic interpolation of log-magnitude you can't reach sub-cent at 46–82 Hz without absurd window lengths (1-cent bins at 46 Hz would need a multi-second FFT); (b) octave errors — the fundamental of a low guitar string is often 10–20 dB below its 2nd/3rd harmonic through a phone mic, so the peak-picker locks onto a harmonic. HPS (harmonic product spectrum) fixes some octave errors but not the resolution problem. FFT is still useful as a cross-check/voting input and for spectrum display, and phase-difference (phase-vocoder) methods can reach high precision, but time-domain YIN/MPM with interpolation is simpler and proven for this job.

**CREPE (neural, MIT license)** — state-of-the-art robustness, beats pYIN/SWIPE on noisy material; operates on 1024-sample frames at 16 kHz; output is 360 pitch classes on a 20-cent grid, refined by weighted averaging to ~±1–2 cents practical. Full model is ~22M params; even "tiny" via TFLite costs far more CPU/battery than YIN for LESS displayed precision than interpolated YIN on the clean, monophonic near-field signal a tuner sees. Overkill for MVP; a possible Pro-phase fallback for very noisy environments.

## 2. Window/buffer sizing, interpolation, update rate

At 48 kHz: F#1 = 46.25 Hz → period ≈ 1038 samples; A1 = 55 Hz → ≈ 873 samples. YIN/MPM as commonly implemented search lags up to N/2, so the buffer must be ≥ 2 periods of the lowest target: ≥ ~2076 samples for F#1. **Practical choice: N = 4096 (85.3 ms @ 48 kHz, 92.9 ms @ 44.1 kHz)** — gives τmax = 2048 > 1038 with margin and ~2–4 periods integrated. N = 8192 improves noise averaging for F#1/B0 territory at the cost of ~171–186 ms of signal per estimate (acceptable for a "precision/low-tuning mode," too sluggish as the only mode).

Raw lag quantization at 48 kHz: one-sample lag error at 46.25 Hz = 1200·log2(1+1/1038) ≈ **1.67 cents**; at 55 Hz ≈ 1.98 cents; at 82.4 Hz (E2) ≈ 2.97 cents. So sub-cent display REQUIRES sub-sample refinement: **parabolic interpolation of the CMNDF minimum (YIN step 5) or NSDF maximum (MPM)** — a standard 3-point quadratic fit — typically improves resolution by 1–2 orders of magnitude, comfortably below 0.1–0.5 cent on stable signals. Both original papers include it; verify any library you adopt actually implements it (a TarsosDSP GitHub issue #113 notes its MPM/FastYin implementations are incomplete vs. the papers and show octave artifacts — another reason not to port them blindly).

**Update rate:** decouple hop from window. With N=4096 and hop 1024–2048 (75–50% overlap) you get ~23–47 estimates/sec at 48 kHz — matching the target 20–40 Hz. Perceived latency is dominated by window length (~85 ms) plus smoothing, not hop; ChromTuner quotes sub-50 ms perceived latency at ~22 Hz updates because the smoothed needle starts moving before the estimate fully settles. Optional CPU saver for a tuner (all fundamentals < 1 kHz): low-pass at ~1–2 kHz and decimate 2–4x (to 12–24 kHz); this cuts FFT cost 4–16x, and parabolic interpolation compensates the coarser lag grid. Keep the input high-pass at ~30 Hz, NOT the 70 Hz some tuners use — 70 Hz would attenuate the very fundamentals (46–55 Hz) this app must nail.

## 3. Open-source implementations and LICENSES (critical for a commercial app)

- **TarsosDSP (Java)** — YIN, FastYin, MPM, AMDF, dynamic-wavelet. **GPL v3 — confirmed** (repo LICENSE; the project itself borrows from GPL'd aubio, so it cannot be relicensed permissively). Unusable in a closed-source commercial app unless you open-source the app or negotiate a commercial license with Joren Six.
- **pitch_detector_dart (pub.dev, techpotatoes)** — pure-Dart YIN only. Declared **BSD-3-Clause, but the README explicitly says the YIN code is "ported from the tarsosDSP library"** — i.e., a derivative of GPL v3 code carrying no GPL notice. The BSD label is very likely invalid (the porter cannot relicense a GPL derivative). **Legal contamination risk — avoid for a commercial app.** Also low activity (last release v0.0.7, April 2024) and no MPM.
- **pitchupdart (pub.dev, BSD-3-Clause, Lucas Bento)** — only the note-mapping/tuning-status layer on top of pitch_detector_dart; itself trivial and clean, but its ecosystem pairs it with the tainted package.
- **flutter_pitch_detection (pub.dev)** — labeled MIT, Android-only, but **uses TarsosDSP as its Android backend → the combined work is GPL v3**. Same contamination problem; avoid.
- **aubio (C)** — mature YIN/YIN-FFT/mcomb/schmitt. **GPL v3+**; the author explicitly invites contact for commercial licensing. Viable only with a paid license or open-sourcing.
- **sevagh/pitch-detection (C++)** — **MIT.** Implements MPM, YIN-FFT, pYIN, pMPM, all O(N log N), with a C API (FFI-friendly). Depends on FFTS (can be swapped for BSD-licensed KissFFT or pffft to keep the dependency chain clean). Best starting point for a native core.
- **cycfi Q (C++)** — **Boost Software License 1.0** (more permissive than MIT — no attribution required in binaries). Header-only DSP with a guitar-focused pitch detector (Joel de Guzman's work is guitar-centric; the old bitstream-autocorrelation detector was replaced by a better algorithm with integrated onset detection). CAVEAT: his 2023–2024 posts state the newest-generation "Hz" pitch-detection core will be **GPL v3 / dual-licensed** — the in-repo Q detector remains BSL-1.0, but verify file-level licenses of the exact version you vendor.
- **CREPE** — **MIT** (code and pre-trained weights); TFLite conversion is documented. Heavy; see §1.
- **c4dm/pyin Vamp plugin** — GPL; avoid.
- Also relevant: **fftea** (pure-Dart FFT, Apache-2.0-style permissive, SIMD-friendly Float64x2List, in-place, 60–80x faster than older Dart FFT packages) and **open_dspc** (Dart FFI DSP package with pffft/pocketfft/kissfft backends, benchmarked on mid-range Android — Exynos 9810 — inside Flutter isolates; shows pffft fastest and big wins from FFT plan reuse).

## 4. Pure Dart vs C++ (dart:ffi) for real-time

Cost model at N=4096, 25–40 updates/s: naive YIN difference function ≈ (N/2)² ≈ 4.2M multiply-adds/frame → ~100–170M MAC/s; Dart AOT with Float32List/Float64x2List typed data sustains this on one mid-range core with headroom (roughly 5–20% of a core), and fftea-accelerated YIN-FFT/MPM drops per-frame cost to a few FFTs of 8k (~1–3 ms/frame on mid-range Android). Verdict: **pure Dart IS fast enough for the MVP at 4096-sample windows and 20–40 fps, provided you (a) use typed lists, (b) use FFT-based autocorrelation (fftea) rather than the naive O(N²) loop for the 8192 window, and (c) run detection in a dedicated isolate fed by the mic stream so the UI isolate never blocks.** At N=8192 naive Dart becomes marginal (~17M MACs/frame), which is exactly where FFT-based computation or a native core matters. A C++ core via dart:ffi from day one is NOT required, but plan the seam: define a Dart `PitchDetector` interface now so an FFI-backed implementation (sevagh/pitch-detection + pffft/KissFFT) can drop in for the Pro phase — buying 5–10x headroom for 8192-sample precision mode, multi-algorithm voting, spectrum display, and lower battery drain. Audio capture itself must be native-fed regardless (e.g., flutter package wrapping AudioRecord/Oboe delivering PCM streams).

## 5. Transient rejection, smoothing, confidence

- **Pick-attack gating:** the first ~30–80 ms after an onset is inharmonic (pick noise, string settling sharp — a freshly picked string reads several cents sharp before decaying to stable pitch). Detect onset via short-term RMS jump (or spectral flux) and suppress/deweight estimates for a fixed hold-off; also gate on absolute RMS floor so silence never produces readings.
- **Confidence metrics:** YIN's minimum CMNDF value d'(τ) is an aperiodicity measure — accept frames only when d'min < ~0.1–0.15 (the canonical 0.1 threshold), tightening or exposing it as "noise gate." MPM's NSDF clarity (0–1) is equivalent — accept > ~0.8–0.93. flutter_pitch_detection likewise exposes a 0–1 confidence threshold; commercial tuners gate on this before moving the needle.
- **Smoothing pipeline used by quality tuners (per ChromTuner's published spec and standard practice):** median-of-5 on accepted estimates (kills single-frame octave/harmonic glitches — a median filter passes only values that persist ≥3 frames), then exponential smoothing (alpha ~0.2–0.4) for the needle, plus display hysteresis: note name switches only when the new note persists N frames, and the "in-tune" indicator uses an enter/exit band (e.g. enter at ±1–2¢, exit at ±3¢) to prevent flicker at boundaries.
- **Octave-error correction for plucked strings:** post-check the candidate against f/2 and 2f — if the CMNDF dip at half the detected frequency's lag is nearly as deep (within ~1.3x), prefer the lower octave; MPM's k-threshold does this implicitly. ChromTuner advertises this as an explicit stage.
- **Bonus for your "tuning stability analysis" feature:** the per-frame (timestamp, f0, confidence) stream from this same pipeline is exactly the input needed — fit pitch drift over 0.5–2 s post-onset and report cents-of-drift, using confidence to weight the fit.

## RECOMMENDATION
**MVP (Android, Flutter): pure-Dart MPM as primary with YIN cross-check, FFT-accelerated via fftea, in an isolate.**
- Algorithm: MPM (NSDF via fftea FFT autocorrelation, k = 0.9, parabolic interpolation) as the primary detector — it was designed for real-time guitar tuning, has the best octave behavior on harmonic-heavy low strings, and gives a free clarity/confidence value. Implement YIN-FFT (threshold 0.1, parabolic interpolation) alongside — from the de Cheveigné/McLeod PAPERS, not from TarsosDSP or pitch_detector_dart code (clean-room keeps the license clean; the papers are not copyrightable) — and use agreement between the two as a high-confidence signal.
- Parameters: capture 48 kHz mono; 30 Hz high-pass (not 70 Hz — you must pass 46.25 Hz); window 4096 samples, hop 1200–2048 (~23–40 updates/s); optional low-pass + decimate-by-2 to halve FFT cost. Add a "low tuning" mode that switches to 8192-sample windows when the target preset is drop A / 8-string (F#1) for extra noise averaging.
- Stability layer: RMS gate → onset hold-off ~50 ms → confidence gate (NSDF clarity > 0.9 / YIN d' < 0.12) → octave sanity check vs f/2 → median-of-5 → exponential smoothing (alpha ≈ 0.3) for the needle → note/in-tune hysteresis. This stack is exactly what ±1-cent commercial tuners document.
- Architecture: define a Dart `PitchDetector` interface from day one; run detection in a dedicated isolate fed by a native-audio-capture plugin's PCM stream. Do NOT depend on pitch_detector_dart, pitchupdart's detector chain, flutter_pitch_detection, TarsosDSP, or aubio (GPL/contamination — see risks).

**Pro phase: C++ core via dart:ffi.**
- Vendor sevagh/pitch-detection (MIT) — MPM + YIN-FFT + pMPM with a ready C API — swapping its FFTS dependency for pffft or KissFFT (both permissive), or write a compact ~500-line C++ MPM/YIN core; expose through the same Dart interface. This buys 5–10x CPU headroom for: continuous 8192-sample sub-cent mode at 40 fps, simultaneous spectrum display, multi-algorithm voting, lower battery, and the tuning-stability drift analysis running concurrently. Same .so serves iOS later via the identical FFI bindings.
- Keep the per-frame (t, f0, confidence) telemetry from the MVP pipeline — it is the direct input to the pitch-drift/stability feature and to auto string detection for the downtuning presets.
- Skip pYIN (GPL reference impl., Viterbi latency, no tuner benefit) and CREPE (MIT but heavy, ~20-cent native grid; reconsider only as an optional noisy-environment assist later).

## RISKS
1. **License contamination is the #1 trap in the Flutter pitch-detection ecosystem.** TarsosDSP is GPL v3 (confirmed). pitch_detector_dart is labeled BSD-3-Clause but its README admits the YIN code is ported from TarsosDSP — a GPL derivative almost certainly mislabeled; shipping it in a closed-source commercial app risks GPL enforcement. flutter_pitch_detection is labeled MIT but bundles TarsosDSP on Android, so the combined work is GPL. aubio and the c4dm pYIN plugin are GPL. If any GPL code path ships, you must open-source the app or buy a commercial license (both TarsosDSP's and aubio's authors entertain commercial licensing on contact).
2. **Clean-room discipline required:** when implementing YIN/MPM in Dart, work strictly from the 2002/2005 papers and MIT-licensed references (sevagh/pitch-detection), never from TarsosDSP/aubio source, and document that provenance.
3. **cycfi Q licensing is shifting:** the main repo is Boost-1.0 today, but Joel de Guzman has stated his next-gen "Hz" pitch-detection core will be GPL/dual-licensed. If you vendor Q's detector, pin the version and audit per-file license headers.
4. **Phone-mic physics at 46–55 Hz:** built-in MEMS mics roll off steeply below ~100 Hz, so the F#1/A1 fundamental may be 10–20 dB under its harmonics — the algorithm sees mostly harmonics, making octave-checking logic and the 8192-sample low-tuning mode load-bearing, and real-device testing on cheap Androids (not just emulators/sine tests) essential. Sub-cent claims must be validated against real recorded low-string samples, not synthetic tones.
5. **Latency/precision tension:** 8192-sample windows (~171 ms) plus median-of-5 means ~0.3–0.5 s to a settled reading on the lowest strings; UX must communicate "settling" rather than showing a jittery needle, or beginners will chase noise with the peg.
6. **Fresh-string / pick-attack sharpness:** strings read several cents sharp immediately after picking; without onset hold-off the ±1-cent display will mislead users — this interacts directly with your stability-analysis feature and needs tuning on real instruments.
7. **Unverified details to confirm during implementation:** exact current file-level licensing inside cycfi/q; sevagh/pitch-detection's FFTS dependency license (swap for KissFFT/pffft regardless); whether TarsosDSP commercial terms are affordable if you ever want its exact code; and pure-Dart CPU figures are engineering estimates from fftea/open_dspc benchmarks (Exynos 9810-class), not measured — prototype the Dart MPM early and profile on a mid-range device before committing.
